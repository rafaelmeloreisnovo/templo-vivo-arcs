## 0. Core thesis: defensible rights, never purported sovereign immunity

The strongest defensible design is a lawful, voluntarily joined, rights-respecting spiritual/scientific/educational and expressive project with documentary provenance, appropriate legal form, rights of exit, individual protections, independent review and provable compliance. A religious/spiritual project is not a state, court, embassy, diplomatic mission or legal exemption by naming or symbolic declaration. The UK does not have a single codified constitution; England and Wales are not interchangeable with all UK legal systems. In the US, the First, Fourth and Fifth Amendments primarily constrain state action; they do not automatically prohibit a private host from moderating content or a private person from criticizing the project.

An opinion is not a ready-made license or an injunction. Case-law arguments require: jurisdiction, standing, ripe concrete harm, state involvement, facts analogous to the holding, controlling vs persuasive authority, subsequent treatment, remedies and legal counsel.

## 2. UK — English law, UK Supreme Court and Strasbourg (not interchangeable)

Legal framework: Human Rights Act 1998, sections 3, 6, 7 and Sch. 1 arts. 6, 8, 9, 10, 11, 14 as applicable, together with Equality Act 2010, data protection, trust/charity/contract and safeguarding law. Section 6 specifically constrains **public authorities** in defined situations; private obligations may arise under other domestic laws or the human-rights duties of courts.

| Authority | Narrow holding | Project use | Adverse or limiting counterargument |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R (Hodkin) v Registrar General**, [2013] UKSC 77 | English law should not apply an unduly narrow, deity-only concept of religion in the *Places of Worship Registration Act* context. | Evidence-based classification of beliefs/practices, if lawful recognition as place of worship is genuinely sought. | Does NOT automatically register, tax-exempt or recognize Templo; application depends on physical place, practices and statutory criteria. |
| **Shergill v Khaira**, [2014] UKSC 33 | Secular courts avoid deciding truth of religious doctrine but can adjudicate objectively ascertainable religious questions needed to decide civil rights, property, trustees and contractual matters. | Separate belief/teaching from institution/property/trust, membership terms, appointment rules and enforceable rights. | Do not claim ecclesiastical self-rule blocks contractual litigation or scrutiny of misconduct. |
| **Bank Mellat v HM Treasury (No 2)**, [2013] UKSC 39 | Structured four-part proportionality: important objective; rational connection; less intrusive options; fair balance. | Use documented decision records when challenging or modeling proportionate restrictions. | Government sanction/bank facts; test is context-specific and **not** universal veto power over regulations. |
| **Eweida and Others v United Kingdom**, ECtHR 2013, apps 48420/10 etc. | Article 9 rights of expression of belief at work require fair balance; Court distinguished four applicants' outcomes including others' rights and safety. | Voluntary creed, accommodation, religious-symbol policy, individual-vs-community balancing. | European Court of Human Rights, **not UK Supreme Court**; not every faith-based refusal prevails. |
| **Campbell v MGN Ltd**, [2004] UKHL 22 | Personal privacy and expression require careful assessment in misuse-of-private-information context. | Protect personal sensitive religious / health / child data, while allowing lawful reporting and public-interest matters. | Publishing truthful, justified matters of public interest is separately considered; not universal censorship of critics. |
| **Lloyd v Google LLC**, [2021] UKSC 50 | Under the historic DPA 1998 claim, representative compensation without proof of individual damage could not proceed as framed. | Record specific data harm and causation; do not infer automatic compensation for any alleged privacy breach. | Older statutory regime and case-specific procedure; do not extrapolate mechanically to UK GDPR. |

### Case-to-contract controls
- **TV-1 Belief/Conscience:** fully voluntary practice; no retaliation for leaving, dissenting, questioning, having another faith, or none; safeguard right of each person (US Free Exercise, UK HRA art.9 / Hodkin).
- **TV-2 State action:** verify actor and government involvement before asserting constitutional claim (Halleck; HRA s.6).
- **TV-3 Security & metadata:** minimization, purpose/retention, counsel-validated lawful-demand procedure; cryptographic hash and provenance chain separate from personal data, and lawful deletion rights preserved (Riley/Carpenter; UK art.8/Campbell).
- **TV-4 Internal fairness:** state reasons, notice when lawful, appeal to humans, allow correction, individual accountability and proportionate sanctions; do not pretend Fifth Amendment judicial process mechanically binds a private platform (Mathews as analogy; Bank Mellat as public-law example).
- **TV-5 Scientific/authorial claims:** separate faith, philosophy, hypothesis, and test; do not assert patents, UK registration or copyright of bare ideas by declaration (existing license policy continues).
- **TV-6 Child safeguard:** minors' welfare and lawful reporting take priority; no hidden adult-minor contact; no unapproved collection of religious data; when a serious risk exists use the relevant legal safeguarding channel.
- **TV-7 Noninterference:** obey valid legal orders after counsel review, contest them lawfully where grounds exist, never conceal/destroy evidence or impersonate public authority.
- **TV-8 Proportionate moderation:** apply viewpoint-neutral safety rules without collective culpability, distinguish protected criticisms from true threats, measure alternatives, record appeal/receipt.
